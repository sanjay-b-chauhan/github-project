He watches what you do, and steps in when it matters.

No warnings up front. Navid works out the pattern first, then comes in at the moment of the action: on the third skipped note, or the instant you press Send on something your manager would read the wrong way. You fix it right there while he watches, and you carry on.

How he decides

1He watches events, not everything. Zero logs the big moments: a note written or skipped, a message or file sent to the manager, a submission, time with no message in a stage, the grade, a level, days active.
2Private slips: Zero covers twice, Navid steps in the third time. Skip a note and press Continue, and Zero writes it for you, marked as written by Zero. The third time Navid comes in by himself: write this one, it is how it sticks.
3Anything your manager will see is checked at Send, the first time. The send is held, the app slides aside, Navid says why in two or three lines, and you fix it in the app while he watches. Then it sends.
4Patterns across scenarios. The same habit in two scenarios becomes a moment after the review, with the why.
5Never mid typing, mid video, or mid presentation. He waits for Continue, for Send, or for the end.

Adding a check

1. The event. Something Zero already logs: a send, a file, a submission, a skipped field, time idle, a grade.
2. The rule. Private and cheap to fix: Zero covers it twice, Navid on the third. Seen by the manager: at Send, the first time.
3. The line. Two or three short sentences in Navid's voice: what he noticed, why it matters, what to do. No praise, no lecture.
4. The fix. One action the learner takes in the app while he watches: rewrite, add, expand, write it down.
5. The answers. Got it, or Why (one more line). Never more than two keys.
