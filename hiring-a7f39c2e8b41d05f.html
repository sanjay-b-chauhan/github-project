Interviewed

PhonePe, prev. One Card, ZEO Interviewed 18 Aug 2026 Gandhinagar, Gujarat

Built an AI tool that predicts where users look, then went and checked it against a university eye-tracking dataset.

Who is this

  • Graduated 2021, started as an engineer at Splunk for about six months before moving into design.
  • Largely self-taught: Adobe XD, Photoshop, Illustrator, Blender. First product-design exposure was a hackathon intraday-trading mock tested with about 30 users.
  • Path: ZEO as founding designer, then One Card for about 2.5 years, then PhonePe from Sept 2025.

Summary

At PhonePe he owns two charters, credit cards and FD/RD, including oversight of 3 of PhonePe's 5 co-branded credit cards. He was clear about which parts of the FD flow he actually designed versus what was fixed by the third-party Upswing SDK, the PhonePe design system and internal audit. On FD and Daily RD he walked through the 70/30 cohort split on the homepage entry point, roughly 30 to 35 percent CTR and conversion on both, and the move to an aggregator page once both performed.

For tier 2 and 3 audiences he designed around safety of money, speed, instant withdrawal and low reading burden, using cues like secured with DICGC, no KYC up to a threshold, and video instead of heavy text.

His clearest UX fix: an FD-backed credit card flow where users could not understand why they had to put money in during a card application. The old screen read as “enter deposit amount” and was driving roughly 12 percent card closure. He reframed it as setting your card limit by creating an FD, and visually tied deposit amount to limit.

The strongest 0 to 1 answer was First Glance, an AI UX-analysis tool he built end to end. It predicts where users look, in what order, where they are likely to click, what design issues exist, and compares version A to B. Runs on Gemini for cost, validated against a university eye-tracking dataset, roughly 1,200 images through four rounds of prompt tuning, landing around 76 percent accuracy against real gaze data.

On AI he has no fixed workflow. PRD first, NotebookLM audiobook to digest, Claude and ChatGPT for brainstorming and early flows, fast prototyping, Leva panel to inspect component states, references from Mobbin, Pinterest and Cosmos. He uses AI for repetitive layout and exploration but keeps craft decisions manual, and does not think AI is there yet for production-grade UI.

Strengths

  • Strong visual taste and craft orientation.
  • Articulates tradeoffs well, especially system constraints versus user value.
  • First Glance shows founder-like initiative, problem selection, user segmentation, technical pragmatism and a real validation instinct.
  • Curious about AI without being dependent on it.
  • Comfortable with ambiguity at least in personal and side-project settings.

Weaknesses and open questions

  • Most company work sits in optimised existing categories rather than genuinely unsolved spaces.
  • Needed prompting before landing on a strong 0 to 1 example.
  • Self-declared preference for perfection over speed, which may bite here.
  • Little evidence of operating through long, messy ambiguity inside a company with no product structure.

Follow-ups for the next round

  • Probe repeatable 0 to 1 behaviour inside ambiguous company settings, not just side projects.
  • Test speed versus perfection when shipping under uncertainty.
  • Explore whether he can design for learning friction, not only delight and clarity.
  • Ask for examples where he owned problem framing, research synthesis, prioritisation under weak data, and post-launch outcomes.

Personality notes

  • Favourite animal: capybara.
  • Builder and storyteller, currently leaning builder. Switches between structure and chaos by context. Works mainly as an IC.
  • Says his superpower is making things visually appealing and rapidly experimenting with new tools.
