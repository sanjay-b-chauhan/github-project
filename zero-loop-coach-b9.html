The coach has one job: earn you skill points.

The coach felt vague because it described Sanjay instead of helping him score. This page covers what we heard in the meeting, what real coaches and coaching products do, and the coach we built from it into b9.

What we heard

From the meeting notes, in the team's words, trimmed.

1The coach helps you get more skill points, so you level up and get the role you want. The manager is who you work for; the coach is on your team.
2It has to be tied to an outcome. A cricket coach does not say "you are doing good". He says hold the bat this way, I want ten hits in a row.
3People open the coach to ask one thing: how do I get more skill points, how do I level up faster.
4It should explain why you got a lot of points, and tell you to stop a habit you repeat: no notes in the problem section, too many ums, asking the manager too much.
5Notifications get skipped. When it matters, the product should pause: blur everything, focus on the coach, Got it, then act.
6Keep the internal scores (communication, independence, quality, speed) for us. No open chat. It may not run in real time, so design for both.

What the best coaches do

We looked at chess, language, speaking, sales, fitness and game coaching, plus the research on feedback, goals and interruptions. Eight findings shaped the design.

They speak in the score

Chess.com labels every move by the win chance it cost. Gong ties talk ratio to deals won. WHOOP turns advice into today's strain range. The note is always a number the user already chases.

Chess.com Game Review, Gong, WHOOP

A count beats an adjective

Specific, hard goals beat "do your best" (effect sizes of .42 to .80 in Locke and Latham). Mobalytics sets "3 of your next 5 games"; Gong gives every stat a range.

Locke and Latham 2002, Mobalytics, Gong

Praise about the person barely works

Praise moved performance by about 0.1 in Hattie and Timperley's review, and more than a third of feedback made things worse when it pointed at the self. Feedback about the task and the next step works.

Hattie and Timperley 2007, Kluger and DeNisi 1996

Pause at a break, never mid-task

Interrupting during a task doubled errors and raised annoyance by up to 106%. Duolingo's video call tutor never corrects you mid-call. Corrections wait for a boundary.

Bailey and Konstan 2006, Duolingo Video Call

Ignorable notes teach people to ignore

In 794,525 phone notifications, only about 15% to 27% of non-message ones got a tap. Alarms that fire too often get tuned out. Pauses work only if they are rare.

Pielot et al. 2018, alarm fatigue studies

Open chat reaches the wrong people

Khanmigo's first version sat beside the work and only helped students who knew when to ask; it now lives inside the problems. Duolingo's Explain My Answer uses fixed follow-ups, not a text box.

Sal Khan on Khanmigo, Duolingo

Show the moment, name what was left

Chess.com shows only the key moves, labels a missed chance as a Miss, and lets you retry that exact position to see what you would have scored.

Chess.com Game Review

Live cues are one word, and rare

Speakers preferred sparse one-word cues to constant feedback. Yoodli and Speaker Coach show a tiny private cue live and a full report after. Live AI help lifted support agents 14%, novices 34%.

Rhema (IUI 2015), Yoodli, NBER w31161

The coach in Zero

Everything below is built into b9 and plays in all three cases.

Jay grades the work. Navid is on your team, and his only job is to get you skill points.

Every line the coach says ends in points or a target. Nothing else ships.

Three targets per scenario, each worth points

The targets are the three things the manager grades, written as things you can count. How many you hit decides the band, so the coach's targets and the manager's grade never disagree.

3,0003 of 3 · Exceptional
2,0002 of 3 · Exceeding
1,0001 of 3 · Meeting
5000 of 3 · finish it
HubSpot routerEvery update tells Jay what is done, what is next, and by when · Back the 2 minutes with a test on real leads before you send · Walk into the meeting ready for the hard question
Notion waterfallTry before you ask · Cover every ask in the brief before you send · Give Esther the cost before she asks
Shopify evalsSend a rough plan inside the first hour · Make the send or hold call yourself, with a reason · 50 test cases, no made up facts

The pill shows what is still possible

Inside a scenario, Navid's side of the pill reads "Up to 3,000". A lost target lowers it, in red, as it happens. A hit raises the floor that is locked in. Every target in the panel shows what it is worth. One slip is a strike; a second slip on the same target loses it.

What the coach answers when you open it

No chat. Three fixed questions sit under Navid's name: Where are my easiest points?, Why did I get my last grade?, What does the next level open? In a scenario the panel shows up to how many points, what is locked in, the three targets with their state and worth, what to keep doing, and one line on how you work. Outside a scenario it answers the question from the meeting directly: the fastest way to the next level, and how many targets you need to hit to get there.

Real time or after each stage

The coach checks at actions, not continuously: when Sanjay presses Send, attaches, submits or asks, which is one check per action. That covers the held send and the strike count. At the end of each stage it reads the stage and updates the three targets. Meetings and presentations are read after, from the transcript. If compute is tight, the per-stage read alone still does the core job.

How it talks

One line, one behavior, one number. Point at the effect on the work, not at the person. No "great job".

"Strike one: Jay had to ask again. One more and clear updates is gone: that is 1,000."A first slip, as a short note with the line to send instead
"Saved: 1,000 kept. That send was your second strike."After the coach held a weak update and Sanjay used the better one
"Target lost: tested before sending. Jay found the missing test, not you. That cost you 1,000."A pause, after the manager's feedback lands
"You earned 1,000. You left 2,000 on the table: the test and the hard question."The post-game, after the review
"No notes in the problem yet. Write down the numbers Jay gives you."A private cue during the kickoff, never a pause
"Clear updates is a habit now, so I will stop reminding you."Fading: a target hit again and again stops being coached

Still to decide

The pause budget. b9 allows at most one pause inside a stage plus the pre-game and post-game. That number is judgment, not research; test it with beta users.
Retry this moment. Chess.com lets you replay the exact position you lost. Zero could let you redo the one message that cost a target, for practice points. Not built.
Habit lock. A target hit three scenarios in a row stops being coached. b9 shows it once, in the best case. The rule for when it locks needs agreeing.
The 1:1 call. The call now opens with an agenda (where you are, what is costing you points, your target next). Whether it stays a video, or becomes the post-game itself, is open.
Live meeting cues. Ums and pace as a one-word private cue during the presentation, or only in the report after.
