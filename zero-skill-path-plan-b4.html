One chain. A learner should be able to say it back after ten seconds.

The rules

Eight rules. The numbers are a proposal sized to today's awards, and they all live in one data file in the prototype, so changing one changes every screen.

One currency: skill points.

Paid for the quality of graded work, each time with a reason. Never for time spent, logins or streaks. That is what lets a recruiter trust the number.

One headline number: the level.

1,000 skill points is a level, at every level. No curve to explain. The level pill is the only number on the home screen.

One solid scenario is about one level.

Below Expectations pays 600, Meeting 1,000, Exceeding 1,300, Exceptional 1,600. You start at Level 1, so Level 10 is about nine scenarios, and better work gets there sooner.

Roles open at some levels, not all.

Level 1 is the start and opens nothing. Level 2 puts the portfolio live. Internships open at Level 3, junior roles at 6, mid level at 8 and 10, senior at 13 and 16. The levels between are the climb, and each one says how far the next roles are.

The job you want is a role like any other.

It sits at a level. The same company also opens earlier doors on the way: an internship at Level 3, a junior role at Level 6.

A role has three states.

Locked, open, applied. The lock is the level and nothing else. For open roles, a match score says how strongly the work backs you.

Everyone starts at Level 1.

There is no Level 0. Onboarding counts for 250 points, so the first bar is already moving. Two scenarios in, the first roles open.

Zero never applies without a yes, and never promises a job.

A level opens a door. The copy says "open to you" and "we apply with you". It never says "guaranteed".

The loop around one scenario

The retention problem is that effort does not feel connected to the job. So every scenario ends on the job, and on a plan to come back.

During

  • Points land at named moments: kickoff notes, saying it back, each stage, the presentation.
  • Every award carries its reason, and what would earn the rest.
  • A running total for this scenario, out of 1,600.

At the end, in this order

  • The band, then what it paid, skill by skill.
  • What was left on the table, and the one fix that wins part of it back.
  • The level. At a gate, the roles that just opened, by name. Between gates, how far the next ones are.
  • "Want me to apply with you?" Nothing goes out without a yes.

Between sessions

  • Pick a time for the next one. It is held in the calendar.
  • One reminder, at that time.
  • On return: what moved on the job side first, then the next scenario.
  • A no from a company names the gap and the scenario that trains it.

The evidence behind the choices

Gathered by web research on 12 October 2026. Figures marked "vendor" are a company's own claim. Where a source could not be opened directly, the claim is left out.

Nobody has put these four things together.

Forage has named-company simulations but gives a completion certificate with no grading, and the employer does not see the work. Hack The Box lets an employer set a minimum rank on a real job listing, in security only. Duolingo has the best habit loop and no jobs. Mercor gates roles on an assessment but has no path to pass it. Forage Hack The Box

Zero's opening: real company work, one skill number, a chosen target, and listings gated by level.

People speed up as the goal gets closer.

In the coffee card study, buyers bought 20 percent faster as they neared the free coffee. A card with two stamps already on it was finished far more often than an empty one needing the same eight purchases (34 against 19 percent, from a secondary source). Kivetz

Always show distance to the named job. Start everyone with 250 points. Pre-fill the next bar at every level.

Rewards for showing up hollow things out.

Across 128 studies, rewards for taking part or merely finishing reduced people's own motivation, while feedback on competence raised it. Duolingo users interviewed about streaks and leagues described chasing points and learning less. Deci Duolingo study

Points are a readout of graded quality, each with a reason. Never for time or logins.

One mechanic at a time.

Duolingo's cleanest streak win was removing a second rule from it: 40 percent more learners on a streak of a week or longer. Their path redesign had one stated goal, to reduce confusion. Duolingo

One currency, one headline number, one next step on the home screen.

A chosen time beats a reminder.

In an online course trial, a commitment device raised completion by 40 percent while reminders alone did nothing. In a 61,000 person gym study, the best single idea was a small bonus for coming back after a missed session. Cornell Megastudy

End each scenario by picking when the next one happens. Build a way back after a miss, not a guilt streak.

A lock must name the way through.

Hack The Box shows the rank a listing needs beside the ones you already qualify for. LinkedIn's Job Match ticks each requirement against your profile. Mercor's reviews show the failure: you pass, then silence. LinkedIn Mercor review

Every locked role states the level, your level, how far, and the scenarios that prove each requirement. Every gate has live roles behind it.

Mass applying does not work. Proof does.

One job seeker's 5,000 bot applications won the same 20 interviews as a few hundred by hand. In Huntr's 2025 data, tailored applications were answered at 5.8 percent against 3.7. Employers now ship limits on applications. Futurism Huntr

Few applications, each tailored, each approved. The portfolio is reordered per role, and the learner sees what goes out.

The first rung is where the squeeze is.

Employment of 22 to 25 year olds in jobs most exposed to AI is 19 percent below less exposed peers. Seven in ten employers say they hire on skills, and screening on grades has fallen from 73 to 42 percent since 2019. Stanford NACE

A level has to mean something to the person hiring: one plain line of what this learner has proven, with the work one click away.

Serious stakes need restraint.

Robinhood dropped its confetti and paid 7.5 million dollars over the way it made trading feel like a game. BloomTech advertised placement rates its own numbers did not support, and was banned from lending. Robinhood BloomTech

One calm celebration when roles open, then stillness. No promise of a job anywhere in the product.

A number is only worth what someone does with it.

LinkedIn retired its Skill Assessments because the people hiring wanted to see skills applied. Duolingo moved to a single score tied to what a learner can do, and LinkedIn now shows it. LinkedIn Duolingo Score

The portfolio shows the level with its meaning, the points per skill, and the scenarios that prove each one.

Decisions this needs from the team

Which levels open roles.

The prototype uses 3, 6, 8 and 10, then 13 and 16. Fewer gates make each one a bigger moment, and they push the first real win later: the first roles now arrive after two scenarios, about three hours of work. Level 2 gives the portfolio going live so that scenario one still lands something. Worth testing against where people drop off today.

What does "open" mean for a role Zero does not control?

The portal already mixes Zero Talent Network roles and external ones. Proposed: the level governs what Zero will put its name behind and apply to with you. It cannot stop someone applying on their own, and the copy never pretends to.

Who sets a role's level?

Proposed: seniority sets the band, and the match engine's own "unlock gaps" set the level inside it. Every requirement should map to a scenario that proves it, which is what the locked role screen shows.

What if the chosen company has no opening the day the level is reached?

The prototype has that state: Zero says so, watches the careers page, and shows what is open at that level today. The moment must never be a dead end.

The numbers.

1,000 a level and 600 to 1,600 a scenario are sized to today's awards (1,250, 1,500 and 2,000). Finance and learning should check them against real completion data. Whatever they become, keep the rule that one solid scenario is about one level.

Learners who already have XP.

Carry it over one for one as skill points and recompute the level on the flat scale. Most people will see their level go up, which is the right direction for a change like this.

The streak.

There are three today. Proposed: one weekly rhythm, sized for scenarios that take one to two hours, with a short task that keeps the week alive after a miss. It pays credits and never skill points.

Scenario content and tracks.

The prototype follows the example in the brief, a product designer aiming at Apple, with stand-in scenario titles and roles. The system does not care about the track: Business Analyst uses the real catalogue as it is.

Voice.

The opening three screens want Navid's voice, as in the tutorial. Nothing was recorded or generated for this build.
